Product Execution · Field Notes

First 90 Days:
Diagnosing and
Fixing Product Execution

In most product organizations, the issue isn't lack of effort — it's lack of clarity. Teams are busy, but not aligned. Everyone is working hard, but not in the same direction.

The Core Problem
Most teams are trying to do too much
The First Job
Diagnose where the system is breaking — then fix it
My Approach
Four phases. Structured. Time-boxed. Outcome-oriented.

Product organizations rarely fail because of effort. They fail because the system doesn't support good decisions that happen quickly.

The Framework

Four phases. Ninety days.

Each phase builds on the last. You can't fix what you haven't diagnosed, and you can't diagnose without first understanding what the business actually needs.

01
Business Context

Understand what the business actually needs

Before evaluating Product, establish what the business needs from it. What is the company trying to achieve — growth, profitability, efficiency? What are the primary constraints — acquisition, retention, monetization, execution? Where is it underperforming?

Output A clear view of what Product should optimize for and how success will be measured
02
System Diagnosis

Evaluate Product as a system

Product is not a collection of individuals — it's a system. I evaluate it across four dimensions: People, Strategy, Process, and Culture. The goal is to understand where execution is breaking, and why.

Output A clear map of where the system is failing and the root causes
03
Gap Identification

Name the systemic issues

Most issues are not isolated — they're systemic. A strategy problem produces execution problems. An ownership problem produces alignment problems. Identifying the root pattern is what makes the fix durable rather than cosmetic.

Output A prioritized list of systemic gaps and their downstream effects
04
Drive Change

A small number of high-leverage changes

Once gaps are clear, I focus on a small number of changes that unlock performance. Not a comprehensive transformation. The things that remove the most friction for the most people, as fast as possible.

Output Teams moving faster with clearer direction and stronger alignment
Phase 02 · In Depth

Diagnosing the product system

Product as a system has four dimensions. Weaknesses in any one of them constrain the whole. These are the questions I ask in each.

People

Do we have the right people?

  • Do we have the right mix of skills — strategy, execution, data, design, engineering partnership?
  • PMs are not interchangeable; success is highly context-dependent. Does each PM match their problem?
  • Who are the highest-leverage contributors? Where are the critical gaps?
Strategy

Is direction clear?

  • Is there a clear strategy tied to business outcomes — or a mission statement masquerading as one?
  • Do teams understand what matters vs. what doesn't?
  • Can any individual on the team explain why their work connects to company goals?
Process

Does the team reliably deliver?

  • Are there clear prioritization and execution rhythms that teams actually use?
  • Is process "just enough" — or is it either absent or suffocating?
  • Do teams have the feedback loops needed to know what's working?
Culture

Is the team outcome-oriented?

  • Is the team focused on outcomes — or on activity? Shipping — or delivering?
  • Are standards genuinely high? Is accountability real?
  • Is failure treated as information, or avoided and hidden?
Phase 03 · In Depth

The most common failure patterns

These systemic issues appear repeatedly across companies at different stages. They rarely exist in isolation — each one amplifies the others.

Pattern 01

Mission masquerading as strategy

There's no clear way to prioritize. Everything can be justified by the "strategy" — which means nothing gets filtered. Teams diverge without realizing it.

Pattern 02

Roadmapping disconnected from goals

Teams are busy but not moving outcomes. Shipping is confused with delivering. The roadmap exists but doesn't connect to what the business actually needs to win.

Pattern 03

Product managers as project managers

Actual product decisions are being made elsewhere — by engineering, sales, or founders. PMs are coordinating work rather than owning direction and outcomes.

Pattern 04

Limited access to timely data

Decision-making is slow or not data-driven because teams can't get the information they need when they need it. Gut replaces evidence. Speed suffers. Quality suffers.

Pattern 05

Unclear ownership and decision rights

Teams don't know what they own or who makes what decisions. Every significant choice requires escalation. Alignment meetings become the product instead of the work.

Pattern 06

Doing too much at once

The team is spread across too many priorities. Nothing gets the focus required to actually move. This often masquerades as execution problems when it's actually a strategy and prioritization failure.

Phase 04 · In Depth

The changes that unlock performance

Once gaps are identified, I focus on a small number of high-leverage interventions. Not everything at once. The things that remove the most friction, fastest.

1

Align leadership and cross-functional teams

Create common understanding of challenges and levers. Get teams working toward the same priorities — not just in the same meetings. Alignment is not agreement in a room; it's consistency in behavior outside of it.

Unlocks

Teams stop re-litigating direction and start moving

2

Define a small set of measurable goals

Focus shifts from "do everything" to "do the right things." Three outcomes tied to business results, owned clearly, with visible progress. Outcomes over activity — always.

Unlocks

A shared definition of winning that drives daily decisions

3

Clarify ownership and decision rights

Every team knows what they own and how success is measured. Decisions that belong at the team level stay there. Escalation is the exception, not the norm.

Unlocks

Speed. The most common bottleneck is unclear ownership, not missing capability.

4

Introduce lightweight operating discipline

Enough structure to drive consistency — without slowing teams down. Rhythm creates reliability. Reliability creates trust. Trust creates the autonomy that high performers need to do their best work.

Unlocks

Predictability and velocity — at the same time

Core Beliefs

Speed comes from clarity, not process

Adding more structure rarely fixes a speed problem. Removing ambiguity almost always does.

Most product problems are system problems

Not people problems. The system either supports good decisions or it doesn't. Fix the system first.

Fix execution by improving how decisions are made

Not by adding more work. Not by adding more process. By making the decisions faster and better.

The Bottom Line

The first 90 days are about
understanding the system
before changing it.

Change before diagnosis produces motion without direction. Diagnosis before change produces clarity before speed. The order matters.
